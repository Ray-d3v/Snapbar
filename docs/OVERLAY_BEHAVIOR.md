# Overlay behavior invariants

The Snapbar control surface is a fixed-size transparent GPUI window whose native Win32 region changes with the visible title-bar UI.

The dimensions below describe the current implementation. For future SaveInfo spacing changes, first improve content and layout within the existing envelope. If sufficient padding still cannot fit, a modest increase of both width and height by approximately the same proportion is allowed; substantial growth along only one axis is not. Keep the title-bar attachment and Teams safe area intact, and update painted, window, and native input geometry together.

- The overlay window follows the center-safe area of the active Teams title bar. Only the defined 20 logical-pixel island drop may cross the caption bottom edge; it must not cover Teams meeting controls or content.
- `DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS` provides the Teams frame in screen coordinates. The occupied caption band is scaled from its fixed 30-logical-pixel design height and bottom-aligned to a validated caption-button bottom so healthy 30-pixel and 46-pixel Teams title bars keep their established Y position. A missing, implausible, or too-short bottom falls back to the nominal band at the frame top. `DWMWA_CAPTION_BUTTON_BOUNDS` limits the usable right side of the title bar; its top and height never define the caption band.
- The fixed 30-pixel caption band reserves its top logical pixel for the DPI-scaled DWM frame outline. The visible idle surface below it is a 92 × 29 logical-pixel transparent title-bar affordance: two adjacent 46-pixel caption-style cells containing only the status dot and a 16-pixel camera glyph. Compact mode is one 46 × 29 logical-pixel camera cell.
- Idle and compact cells stay completely inside the caption band. They have no backplate, shadow, label, rounded bottom, or drop; the sampled title-bar material extends below the caption during native hover disclosure.
- After the configured disclosure delay, the surface expands inside a fixed 288 × 50 logical-pixel envelope. Its top row remains transparent for the DWM outline, leaving a 288 × 49 visible island made from 29 caption-contained pixels plus the unchanged 20-pixel drop. The caption-contained portion remains visually transparent even when expanded; only the growing drop below the caption is opaque and uses the sampled Teams title-bar color. Text and controls use that same color to select their contrast palette; the material never transitions to forced black. Its shoulders follow a concave curve with 8 logical-pixel depth and a 24 logical-pixel inset, joining within the 20-pixel drop; DPI rounding must keep the shoulder and bottom corner from interfering, and the bottom corners use a 12 logical-pixel radius.
- All presentation modes use a luminance-derived palette: dark foregrounds on light surfaces and light foregrounds on dark surfaces. Semantic capture and status colors retain their meaning in both palettes.
- The caption's lower separator follows the growing island's concave shoulders, sides, and bottom. The opaque drop masks the old straight line inside the island; the caption itself stays transparent. Separator strips use the same quantized progress, physical scanlines, and DPI rounding as the native region, without a separate curve or a per-frame buffer. The background, sampled separator, and its physical row offset are published atomically as one material so light and dark themes cannot mix during an update. Sampling examines the nominal caption bottom and the following two logical pixels because the visible Teams separator can lie below the DWM caption-button bottom. The first row differing from the caption needs agreement from at least three points and a majority of visible boundary samples; without consensus it uses the current background color. The stroke starts at that measured row, leaving the caption-colored rows above it unstroked.
- Convert `DWMWA_CAPTION_BUTTON_BOUNDS` from window-relative coordinates using `GetWindowRect`, then validate it against screen-space `DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS`. Use its left edge for horizontal exclusion and only a plausible bottom edge as the vertical anchor; ignore its top and reported height, which may change under maximization and DPI rounding. Position from the maximum expanded surface and reject any placement whose fixed caption-contained height plus the defined drop cannot contain the surface.
- The visible top anchor remains one logical pixel below the caption top throughout disclosure. The idle surface occupies the lower 29 pixels of the caption band. In the default HoverIsland mode, the same spring progress first opens the caption width with cubic easing; after progress `0.12`, the lower edge grows with smoothstep through the 20-pixel drop. The actual Teams caption remains visible through a one-alpha hit surface tinted with its sampled color; the opaque extension below it keeps that same sampled color throughout disclosure. One camera glyph stays at the same logical X anchor while its center moves with the lower edge from `Y=14.5` to `Y=23.5`; its circular capture background appears during that motion. Auxiliary controls follow the lower edge and move between a compact grouping around the camera and their final row positions, with their own fade independent of the description. Collapse follows the same progress in reverse. A fixed, non-interactive context panel leads the horizontal action row: it shows the status dot and meeting state at rest, shows the first hovered control's two-tier action label immediately, and switches to another control's label only after 160 ms of continuous hover. Leaving a button, including entering a gap, immediately cancels the pending hint switch; the currently displayed hint remains until island collapse or page reset, and gaps have no click targets. The SaveInfo one-second dwell is based on actual Save-button hover and is cancelled immediately when Save is left. The owned content-only debounce task is also cancelled when the page, disclosure, compact, target, or quit state changes. The context panel has no outer rounded background; the capture detail remains a keycap-style `Ctrl+Alt+S` shortcut. Capture, save, rescan, and exit actions remain on the same row; no separate tooltip window or downward menu opens.
- The idle camera glyph and expanded capture button share one logical X anchor. Context controls may disclose around that anchor, but the primary capture target must not move horizontally across the transition.
- In the default attachment, one status dot persists at full opacity through disclosure and moves between the idle position and a reserved context status slot. It remains visible when descriptions change and has no handoff between idle and expanded copies. The single camera glyph stays 16 logical pixels and does not fade on hover or disclosure; only its circular plate changes size, contracting to zero with the lower-edge deformation on collapse. Press feedback is separate. Inline and presenter presentations retain their existing behavior.
- In the default attachment, auxiliary save, rescan, and exit glyphs use a separate opacity path from the description text. They follow the same lower-edge motion in both directions; during collapse they rise toward the title bar and move slightly inward toward the capture anchor while fading over a longer tail, finishing their fade near the caption band. The default closing spring uses stiffness 450 and damping 40 so the return remains visible; opening keeps stiffness 900 and damping 46. Both directions derive icon positions and opacity from the shared progress, preserving continuity on reversal.
- Selecting the default island's exit glyph replaces the controls in the same 288 × 50 island with the two-line `Snapbarを` / `終了しますか？` prompt and one horizontal `終了` / `キャンセル` row. Only explicit `終了` confirms process exit; cancel, hover dismissal, hiding, compact fallback, and target changes restore or dismiss confirmation, while the tray exit remains direct even when the island is hidden.
- Hovering the save control continuously for 1 second replaces the controls across the same 288-pixel-wide island with SaveInfo while retaining the normal 288 × 50 envelope, 288 × 49 visible surface, 304 × 52 HWND, and native region; inline retains its 29-pixel height, while presenter attachment retains its existing 49-pixel expanded height. SaveInfo uses a centered two-row content block narrowed to 204 logical px: its upper row has a small left Back arrow, the PNG保存 label, and a directly operable ON/OFF switch; its lower row shows the full Windows Screenshots known-folder path freshly resolved in a background task each time it opens. The path is centered when it fits and stays on the same-height row with horizontal scrolling for long paths. The switch remains on SaveInfo, updates the setting in place, and preserves an in-flight path task. A save click from the normal controls page still toggles the setting and restarts the 1 second dwell. SaveInfo performs no folder creation or copy; the hover disclosure state machine and shared native region writer remain unchanged, and the transition uses only a short 140 ms fade.
- SaveInfo is also cancelled when the overlay is hidden, falls back to compact mode, or changes target, returning to the normal controls when the island is available again.
- Expanded actions retain 30 × 30 logical-pixel hit cells while rendering their visible backplates at 28 × 28. Save, rescan, and exit have no background at rest and a 28-pixel circular background only while hovered. Save ON uses a green folder icon plus a small status dot. The one-pixel inset on adjacent cells increases the perceived gap from 6 to 8 pixels without reducing the click target or changing the capture anchor. The default hover-island row is lifted one pixel so the curved lower body has more breathing room; inline placement keeps its existing vertical anchor.
- When the title-bar safe span is too narrow for the full strip, only the centered camera control is exposed; the compact camera remains a stateful, directly clickable control.
- The native region must be expressed in window-relative coordinates, including the client-area origin offset.
- Reaching a disclosure endpoint requests one native repaint even if pixel rounding already produced the final region on an earlier frame. Identical settled updates remain no-ops; newly exposed pixels must not wait for a later control hover to appear.
- `ClientToScreen` is bound directly from `user32` so the client origin can be converted without depending on a generated windows-rs module placement.
- The native region matches the current visible surface: a rectangular 92 × 29 idle region, rectangular 46 × 29 compact region, or square-top/curved-shoulder/rounded-bottom 288 × 49 expanded island inside the fixed 288 × 50 envelope. A one-alpha render surface keeps the transparent caption cells hit-testable; the reserved frame row and all other pixels outside the native region remain click-through so Teams keeps drawing its outline and title-bar dragging still works.
- Expansion begins only after a stable hover. Collapse occurs after the pointer remains outside the island silhouette for the configured delay.
- GPUI drives the spring for both directions. A normal run preserves its velocity, but a hover reversal first maps the visible gain-2 overshoot back to the spring's underlying position, then reverses direction from that position. Repeated reversals therefore cannot double-amplify the presentation bulge or create a two-stage pause. Expansion reaches its controls in roughly 150–200 ms, permits a bounded 4.4% horizontal presentation overshoot inside the fixed 304 × 52 caption layout viewport, and settles by roughly 300 ms without exceeding the 20-pixel vertical drop. Painting and `SetWindowRgn` consume the same quantized progress.
- The no-activate overlay disables GPUI's default inactive-window frame interval. Animation frames therefore follow compositor-vsync opportunities instead of being capped at roughly 30 fps; the actual rate remains bounded by the active display and DWM.
- A window-level Win32 disclosure controller installed with `SetWindowSubclass` is the source of truth. It owns one explicit Collapsed / ExpandPending / Expanded / CollapsePending state machine, uses `TrackMouseEvent` / `WM_MOUSELEAVE`, and verifies the pointer with `WindowFromPoint` at timer boundaries. GPUI only renders the published mode.
- The expanded-only safety timer is a bounded fallback for missed leave messages; never restore permanent cursor polling.
- Title-bar color sampling runs on a dedicated `ColorSampler` thread owned by the window follower. Its request and result channels each have capacity one, only one narrow-band `BitBlt` into a reusable 32bpp top-down DIB/memory DC may be in flight, and background points plus up to eight columns of lower-edge samples are read from that buffer after `GdiFlush`; full-screen copies and image files are prohibited. Multiple boundary points must agree before using a sampled separator; otherwise the current background color is used for both. The DIB and memory DC are reused while the sampled geometry is unchanged. Native-region and UI updates never wait for sampling. Results are applied only while the title-bar attachment is visible and the target window and DPI-scaled caption height still match the request. The 50 ms settle delay and 350 ms post-read interval remain, while a new hover after settling proceeds without waiting for an older read. Shutdown closes the request channel and joins the sampler off the UI thread. Permanent pointer polling and a second hover state machine remain prohibited.
- The UIA client is reused on its owning thread, but meeting and sharing evidence is inspected afresh on every scan. Each scan uses `FindAllBuildCache` to bulk-fetch Name, AutomationId, ControlType, and IsOffscreen for every result, with cache scope `Element` and a raw-view filter; no element snapshot survives the scan. Live references and the original query remain available as compatibility fallbacks when a provider cannot supply a cached property or bulk request. Process-name caching is keyed by PID and process creation time only for immutable executable version metadata; processes without enumerated windows are pruned and failed lookups are retried, so cached names never become cached meeting decisions.
- WinEvent foreground, minimize, native-window lifecycle, and geometry changes remain prompt signals. Fine-grained client, caret, selection, and focus events do not trigger a full UIA scan; the existing approximately 700 ms watchdog and join/leave debounce values remain in force.
- The bulk UIA query excludes offscreen elements and elements with both empty Name and empty AutomationId, matching the exclusions already made by the evidence classifiers. A populated nonmeeting tree with visible named/identified buttons or text returns its negative evidence without an extra provider warmup scan. Bare or empty trees still use the existing point warmup and retry. Ordinary controls prove only that the provider responded; they never count as meeting or sharing evidence.
- Each sampled point must belong to the target Teams window before and after its pixel read. Reads spanning a caption geometry change are discarded, preventing overlapping windows and movement from supplying a mismatched island color.
- The subclass returns `MA_NOACTIVATE` for `WM_MOUSEACTIVATE`; operating the Snapbar controls must not activate the overlay or take focus from Teams.
- Collapsed, expanded, and compact dimensions come from one shared geometry source, and only the follower worker writes the native Win32 region.
- All declared dimensions are logical pixels. GPUI's per-monitor DPI transition resizes the HWND, and the native geometry derives the matching physical surface, curve, and hit region from the current client dimensions. Raw monitor resolution and physical panel size never select an independent island scale.
- There is no click-to-pin state that can leave the strip permanently open.
- Target-window `LOCATIONCHANGE` and move/size lifecycle notifications wake the bounded follower channel directly; the existing 100 ms follow cadence (8 ms for presenter attachment) remains the fallback when notifications are missed. The callback only signals work, while the single follower worker performs position updates; a pure `LOCATIONCHANGE` for the followed window does not repeatedly trigger meeting UIA scans, and move/size completion still uses the existing meeting scan, watchdog, and debounce paths.
- `--inline-titlebar` selects a second presentation: inside the same fixed 30-pixel caption band, the visible controls disclose on native hover from 92 × 29 to 288 × 29 logical pixels. Like the default island it reserves the top logical pixel for the DPI-scaled DWM frame outline, but it has zero island drop and a rectangular native region. The same window-level hover controller handles entry and collapse, so leaving the visible surface returns it to the collapsed 92 × 29 state.
- Inline disclosure uses a deliberately paced, slightly underdamped spring. Its centered width reveal is visibly in flight at 100 ms and reaches the full row at roughly 180 ms, while the controls cross-fade and fan out continuously from a tight center grouping instead of waiting behind a late visibility threshold. A small bounded contraction supplies the final settle without changing the title-bar-only silhouette.
- Inline disclosure clamps its spring presentation at the 288-pixel target instead of using the island's horizontal overshoot, so the full row cannot intrude into the caption-button exclusion span.
- The title-bar overlay and successful-capture flash keep `WS_EX_TOOLWINDOW` and `WS_EX_NOACTIVATE` and remain topmost relative to Teams. Debug and release launches apply `WDA_NONE` to both windows by default so disclosure motion and the white flash appear in screen sharing and recordings. `--exclude-overlay-from-capture` explicitly applies `WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE` to both; the existing `--recordable-overlay` argument remains a compatible no-op. Snapbar still temporarily excludes the overlay while reading a locally shared monitor so its own screenshot does not contain the controls.
